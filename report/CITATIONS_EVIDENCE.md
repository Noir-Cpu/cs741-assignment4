# Evidence for the additional citations in references.bib

Every entry listed here was checked against CrossRef (`https://api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>`), a publisher or proceedings page (read directly or through an Internet Archive copy of that page), or the paper itself. CHECK results of `scripts/verify_bib.py` are explained per entry below (no CrossRef record, or CrossRef stores the author or title differently). The "Established" line is taken from the abstract or the publisher page; cite the work only for that.

## Statistics

**holm1979** (Holm, S., Scandinavian Journal of Statistics 6(2), 65-70, 1979). No CrossRef record (the guessed DOI 10.2307/4615733 does not resolve at CrossRef or doi.org). Verified from the paper itself: the JSTOR cover page and first page of a scan give "Holm, S.; A Simple Sequentially Rejective Multiple Test Procedure; Scandinavian Journal of Statistics 6(2) (1979), pp. 65-70", published by Wiley for the Board of the Foundation of the Scandinavian Journal of Statistics. The Dem{\v s}ar (2006) reference list (JMLR 7:1-30) gives the same volume, pages and year. Established (abstract): a simple, widely applicable sequentially rejective multiple test procedure (hypotheses rejected one at a time until no further rejection is possible), with a prescribed significance level protecting against type I error for any combination of true hypotheses. The abstract does not use the word "Bonferroni"; do not say the paper defines "Holm-Bonferroni".

**iman1980** (Iman, R. L. and Davenport, J. M., Communications in Statistics - Theory and Methods 9(6), 571-595, 1980). CrossRef, doi:10.1080/03610928008827904: authors, journal, volume 9, issue 6, pages 571-595, year 1980 match. CrossRef and the Taylor and Francis page both misprint the title as "...of the fbietkan statistic". The correct title "Approximations of the critical region of the Friedman statistic" is taken from the reference list of Dem{\v s}ar (2006), JMLR 7 (pages 571-595, 1980). The journal name is the CrossRef form. Established (abstract, publisher page): compares two new approximations with the usual chi-square and F large-sample approximations for the critical region of the Friedman test (randomized complete block design); the usual chi-square approximation is described as grossly inaccurate for most commonly used (k, b). The abstract does not state the F-statistic formula; for the formula and the statement that Friedman's statistic is conservative, cite Dem{\v s}ar (2006), who describes it.

**garcia2008** (Garc{\'\i}a, S. and Herrera, F., JMLR 9:2677-2694, 2008). JMLR has no DOI. Publisher page https://jmlr.org/papers/v9/garcia08a.html and the PDF header agree on authors, title, volume 9, pages 2677-2694, year 2008. verify_bib reports CHECK because CrossRef has no record. Established (abstract and text): extends Dem{\v s}ar (2006) to all pairwise comparisons of n x n classifiers; the text names the Nemenyi test as the test for all pairwise comparisons after Friedman (citing Nemenyi 1963) and proposes more powerful procedures.

**Nemenyi test, other published source.** Not found. Nemenyi (1963) is an unpublished Princeton PhD thesis (as listed in Dem{\v s}ar 2006), which WR23 excludes. P. Nemenyi, "Variances: An elementary proof and a nearly distribution-free test", The American Statistician 23(5), 35-37, 1969 (doi:10.1080/00031305.1969.10481896) is a different paper and was not read for this claim; do not cite it for the Nemenyi post-hoc test. Cite Dem{\v s}ar (2006) and Garc{\'\i}a and Herrera (2008) as the published descriptions of the test.

## Ensemble methods

**breiman1996** doi:10.1007/BF00058655 (CrossRef: Machine Learning 24(2), 123-140, 1996; a duplicate record 10.1023/A:1018054314350 exists). Established (Springer abstract): bagging generates multiple versions of a predictor from bootstrap replicates of the learning set and aggregates them (average for numerical outcomes, plurality vote for classes); it gives substantial accuracy gains when the predictor is unstable.

**breiman2001** doi:10.1023/A:1010933404324 (Machine Learning 45(1), 5-32, 2001). Established (abstract): a forest is a combination of tree predictors, each depending on a random vector sampled independently with the same distribution; the generalization error converges almost surely as trees are added and depends on the strength of the individual trees and the correlation between them; random feature selection at each split compares favorably to Adaboost and is more robust to noise.

**freund1997** doi:10.1006/jcss.1997.1504 (Journal of Computer and System Sciences 55(1), 119-139, 1997). Established (ScienceDirect abstract): a decision-theoretic on-line learning model using the multiplicative weight-update rule, and from it a new boosting algorithm that needs no prior knowledge of the weak learner's performance, with extensions to multi-class and real-valued targets.

**friedman2001** doi:10.1214/aos/1013203451 (The Annals of Statistics 29(5), 2001). CrossRef has no page numbers; pages 1189-1232 are from the Project Euclid page (citation_firstpage 1189, citation_lastpage 1232). Established (abstract): function estimation viewed as numerical optimization in function space, a gradient-descent boosting paradigm for additive expansions under any fitting criterion, with TreeBoost for regression trees; connected to the boosting of Freund and Schapire.

**ho1998** doi:10.1109/34.709601 (IEEE TPAMI 20(8), 832-844, 1998). CrossRef lists the author as "Tin Kam Ho" in a single name field, hence CHECK; the entry uses "Ho, T. K.". Established (abstract): a forest of trees built in randomly chosen subspaces of the feature vector (pseudorandom selection of feature subsets); shown to beat single-tree classifiers and other forest methods on public datasets; discusses independence between trees.

**geurts2006** doi:10.1007/s10994-006-6226-1 (Machine Learning 63(1), 3-42, 2006). Established (Springer abstract): a tree ensemble that strongly randomizes both attribute and cut-point choice when splitting a node (Extra-Trees); in the extreme case totally randomized trees whose structure is independent of the output; a parameter tunes the randomization strength; main strength is computational efficiency besides accuracy.

**kuncheva2003** doi:10.1023/A:1022859003006 (Machine Learning 51(2), 181-207, 2003). Established (Springer abstract): studies ten diversity statistics (four averaged pairwise and six non-pairwise) in four experiments; the results raise doubts about the usefulness of diversity measures in building classifier ensembles in real pattern recognition problems.

**wolpert1992** doi:10.1016/S0893-6080(05)80023-1 (Neural Networks 5(2), 241-259, 1992). Established (ScienceDirect abstract): introduces stacked generalization, which learns in a second space whose inputs are the guesses of the original generalizers on held-out parts of the learning set; described as a more sophisticated version of cross-validation.

**dietterich2000** doi:10.1007/3-540-45014-9_1 (Multiple Classifier Systems, MCS 2000, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 1857, Springer, pp. 1-15, 2000). CrossRef and the Springer chapter page give title, pages, year and "LNCS vol 1857". The page names the venue "International Workshop on Multiple Classifier Systems"; the ordinal ("First") was not on the page, so the booktitle omits it. Established (abstract): reviews ensemble methods (Bayesian averaging, error-correcting output coding, bagging, boosting), explains why ensembles can outperform a single classifier, reviews comparison studies and adds experiments on why AdaBoost does not overfit rapidly.

## Imbalance, boosting systems and cost sensitivity

**chawla2002** doi:10.1613/jair.953 (JAIR 16, 321-357, 2002). Established (abstract): SMOTE over-samples the minority class by creating synthetic minority examples; combined with under-sampling of the majority class it gives better classifier performance in ROC space than under-sampling alone, evaluated with C4.5, Ripper and Naive Bayes using AUC.

**chen2016** doi:10.1145/2939672.2939785 (Proceedings of the 22nd ACM SIGKDD International Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining, 785-794, 2016). CrossRef gives title "XGBoost" with subtitle "A Scalable Tree Boosting System", hence CHECK. Established (abstract): a scalable end-to-end tree boosting system with a sparsity-aware algorithm for sparse data and a weighted quantile sketch for approximate tree learning, plus cache-access, compression and sharding insights.

**ting2002** doi:10.1109/TKDE.2002.1000348 (IEEE TKDE 14(3), 659-665, 2002). CrossRef lists the author as "Kai Ming Ting" in one name field (CHECK); entry uses "Ting, K. M.". Established (abstract): an instance-weighting method that generalizes standard tree induction so that initial instance weights determine whether minimum-error or minimum-high-cost-error trees are induced; empirically better than the original algorithm in total misclassification cost, number of high-cost errors and tree size on two-class data, and simpler and more effective than the altered-priors method.

**elkan2001** (Elkan, C., Proceedings of the Seventeenth International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 973-978, 2001). No DOI. The IJCAI proceedings page https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/2001-2/ (volume 2) lists the title "Proceedings of the Seventeenth International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence" and the Machine Learning and Data Mining section starting at page 815; the section PDF https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/01/IJCAI-2001-k.pdf contains the paper starting on printed page 973 and ending on printed page 978 (the next page is 979). The authors' copy (cseweb.ucsd.edu/~elkan/rescale.pdf) says "To appear, Proceedings of the Seventeenth ... (IJCAI'01)". Publisher omitted (not confirmed). Established (abstract): when different errors cost different amounts it can be optimal to act as if a class is true even when another is more probable; characterizes reasonable cost matrices; for two classes, a theorem on changing the proportion of negative examples to get cost-optimal decisions; argues that rebalancing has little effect on standard Bayesian and decision tree learners, so the recommendation is to learn from the training set as given and compute optimal decisions explicitly from the classifier's probability estimates.
